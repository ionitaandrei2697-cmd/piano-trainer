Your hand & fingering

The suggested fingers are worked out for the hand you describe here.

Try the stretch on the keys: thumb on a C, how far up can the little finger reach and still press a key cleanly? The hand span in centimetres (thumb tip to little-finger tip, spread flat on a table) is turned into an interval by a rule of thumb (a white key is 2.35 cm wide) — the interval you can actually play is the better guide. Shifts are jumps of the whole hand, passes thumb-unders and finger-overs, stretches spans past what is comfortable for your hand, and a wavy line on a falling chord means it is wider than you can hold: roll it, or take the far note with the other hand.

How strictly a note is judged, and what happens after a clean pass.

Required means a note is not correct until it has been held for its written length: in Follow a clipped or overheld note comes off the accuracy; in Wait the playhead does not move on until the chord has been held for most of its value, and letting go early means striking it again.

Metronome

The Metronome button in the toolbar turns the click on and off; this is what it sounds like.

Beats per bar follows the score's time signature unless you pick one — useful for a MIDI file that carries a wrong one. Subdivide adds softer clicks between the beats (“and” / “și” when counting in 2), which is what makes a dotted rhythm or a triplet easy to place.

Sound & keyboard

MIDI keyboard
Hands-free

Choose a key you never play — the top or bottom key of the keyboard is usual. It stops making a sound and controls the app instead, so you can start, pause and repeat without reaching for the screen. A keyboard with Start / Stop buttons works without setup.

Tap along with the clicks — any key, a MIDI key, or this pad.

Wireless headphones hear everything late. Without this, Follow mode marks every note you play in time with the sound as late. Measure it plays eight clicks for you to tap along to; the falling notes, the cursor and the scoring are then shifted to match what you hear.

The keyboard plays like a piano

WERTYUIOP
ASDFGHJKL;'
CDEFGABCDEF

The home row is the white keys; the row above holds the sharps, each one sitting in the gap it divides. R and I stay silent, because a piano has no black key between E–F or B–C — that gap is what lets you find your place by touch. The reachable stretch is marked in green under the on-screen keys, and Z / X move it an octave at a time. Keys are read by position, so the layout is the same on a Romanian, German or French keyboard.
